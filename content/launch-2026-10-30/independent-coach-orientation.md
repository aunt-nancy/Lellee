# Independent Coaching orientation — review draft

This draft implements the already-required orientation as a reviewable product
walkthrough. It is not an approved course, an issued certificate, accreditation,
professional licensure, or permission to serve clients. No fee, duration,
checkout or additional specialty requirement is established here. The proposed
training mapping is in `coach-training-mapping.json`.

## 1. Know which workspace you are using

Independent Coaching has its own account entry and practice workspace within
the shared Lellee architecture. Consumer journeys and independent practice
operations have different purposes and permissions. Signing in to Coaching
does not authorize access to a consumer's private journal, messages, history or
other programs. Future employee/learning coaching remains a separate lane.

Practice exercise: identify the Coaching sign-in, Practice Setup, Credentials &
Training and Quick Start areas. Describe which information belongs to your
practice and which remains the client's private information. Use a test fixture
for the exercise; do not create a customer account or send an invitation.

## 2. Separate setup from permission to provide services

Account creation, learning, business profile preparation and draft offers can
remain available during onboarding. Client-facing work requires current
verified orientation, onboarding certification, approved business/program scope
and the specific coach's service authorization. Membership in a practice, a
dashboard status card or an approved owner's account does not qualify another
coach. A failed approval lookup never becomes permission to begin services.

Practice exercise: explain what a new coach, an approved coach and a coach with
expired or revoked evidence may do. Include draft preparation, client invitations,
active offers, scheduling, messages and assignments. Explain why an interface
button cannot override the server's authorization decision.

## 3. Describe your role accurately

Coaching can support participant-chosen goals, practical organization,
accountability, reflection and manageable next steps. Do not imply clinical,
medical, legal, benefits-eligibility, housing-placement or emergency authority
through ordinary coaching. Do not describe a Lellee completion certificate as
a professional license. Questions outside your role go to an appropriate
qualified professional, public screener or verified nonprofit navigator as
applicable. Referral does not automatically transmit a person's records.

Practice exercise: rewrite a service description to name a bounded coaching
purpose. Identify a request outside that purpose and describe the appropriate
kind of outside help without diagnosing, determining eligibility or offering
individualized legal advice.

## 4. Use explicit consent and narrow sharing

A client relationship and the client's acceptance are separate from permission
to share particular information. Each person should understand the proposed
sharing scope and be able to change or revoke it. A coach, supporter, family
member or sponsor does not automatically receive a private account history.
Group membership does not grant access to other groups or another client's
individual appointment. Keep operational notes limited to their proper purpose.

Practice exercise: prepare a fictional transport-help request using only details
needed for that task. Separately identify information that should remain private.
Explain the difference between relationship acceptance and permission to share a
journal-derived summary. Describe how a client can decline or end sharing.

## 5. Prepare an offer without activating payment

An unapproved coach can prepare a draft service. Current verified authorization
is required to activate service delivery, and additional consent/payment rules
still apply. Consumer coaching prices and independent-practice platform plans
are different offers. This orientation sets no price or fee and authorizes no
marketplace, payment or payout activation. Existing approved commercial decisions
remain unchanged.

Practice exercise: prepare one fictional draft offer with a clear purpose and
scope. Identify the independent authorization, client consent, pricing disclosure
and payment-readiness checks that must precede any real paid session. Do not
send a checkout, enroll a client or collect money for the exercise.

## 6. Keep evidence and changes traceable

Training or credentials submitted by the coach are self-reported until reviewed
through the authorized process. Coaches cannot verify their own evidence or issue
their own onboarding certificates. Current authorization depends on the exact
coach, practice, program, requirement version and underlying evidence, including
expiry and revocation. Monthly ethics reminders retain their existing
informational policy; they are not a substitute for onboarding qualification.

Practice exercise: identify the difference between a submitted training record,
verified completion, an issued certificate and service authorization. Explain why
an expired certificate still displaying an old active label must not authorize new
service work. State the factual correction path for an inaccurate evidence record.

## Knowledge check for review

1. A new coach can open the dashboard. Can they invite a real client? Explain why
   dashboard access and service authorization differ.
2. The owner is approved but a second coach has not completed orientation. Whose
   qualification must be checked for the second coach's session?
3. A client accepts a relationship. Does that acceptance expose all journals?
4. A coach belongs to Group A. Can a Group A member see Group B's schedule?
5. An approval lookup fails. Should an offer be saved active or draft?
6. An onboarding certificate is revoked while the browser is open. Can the old
   page or token authorize the next service request?

Review key: no service rights from dashboard access; check the assigned and acting
coach; no automatic journal sharing; no cross-group schedule access; fail closed
to draft preparation; recheck current evidence on the next sensitive request.
The reviewer must assess explanations and practical demonstrations, not count
the presence of these words as proof of competence.

## Review and evidence requirements

Use existing in-house course, source, curriculum, assessment, scope/privacy,
capstone and verification controls as applicable. Preserve their qualification,
attestation and audit requirements. This draft does not waive a review slot,
appoint a reviewer, invent a signoff or enable an administrator override.

Existing topic references: Foundations modules `scope_role_boundaries`,
`ethics_privacy_consent` and `documentation_shared_progress`. Product behavior
must be checked against the exact deployed authorization/consent implementation
before this orientation can claim to describe live behavior. Current corrections
remain on the review branch.
