# Coaching Foundations — practice workbook draft

Review-only companion to the eight existing Foundations modules. These are
original fictional teaching examples, not client records or validated assessments.
They supply the concrete cases requested by the existing practice requirements.
They do not change course hours, pass marks, prices, reviewer qualifications or
the existing capstone rubric. The course remains unpublished pending review.

Work in a test workspace or on a fictional worksheet. Do not send invitations,
contact a participant, copy a private journal or activate a service to complete
these exercises. The instructor must identify the reviewed, versioned safety,
privacy and record-correction procedures before using the workbook with learners.
If a procedure is missing, record the gap rather than inventing organizational
policy. No exercise authorizes diagnosis, legal interpretation or treatment.

## 1. Scope and role boundaries

For each request, classify the work as coaching, coaching alongside an appropriate
referral, or work outside coaching. Write one sentence to the participant, one
next action they can choose, and any referral needed. Explain your reasoning.
Context may change the answer; naming a category alone is insufficient.

| Case | Fictional request |
|---|---|
| S1 | “Help me break my job search into two steps I can try this week.” |
| S2 | “Tell me whether this eviction notice is legally valid.” |
| S3 | “Help me list the questions I want to ask a tenant legal-aid service.” |
| S4 | “Diagnose why I cannot concentrate.” |
| S5 | “Help me prepare questions for my clinician about fatigue.” |
| S6 | “Should I stop my prescribed medication before work?” |
| S7 | “Choose the recovery pathway I must follow.” |
| S8 | “Help me compare the practical demands of support options I found.” |
| S9 | “Guarantee that I qualify for this housing benefit.” |
| S10 | “Help me make a checklist of documents the public screener requests.” |

Then rewrite this fictional offer: “Licensed-style therapy and guaranteed housing
approval from a certified Lellee expert.” Use only the qualification actually
earned and a bounded coaching purpose. Identify the misleading claims removed.

Reviewer anchors: S1/S3/S5/S8/S10 can support participant-led organization;
S2/S4/S6/S9 require appropriately qualified interpretation or decisions;
S7 must preserve participant choice. Referral is not permission to transmit data.
A service description cannot turn a course certificate into a professional license.

## 2. Ethics, privacy and consent

Draft a coaching-agreement checklist covering role, purpose, communication,
financial arrangements where applicable, records, sharing, privacy limits,
ending the relationship and how to report a concern. Do not invent legal terms
or modify the approved agreement. Identify where each topic is explained there.

For each case below, identify the purpose, recipient, minimum necessary
information, permission needed and a safe alternative. State what must not be sent.

| Case | Proposed action |
|---|---|
| P1 | An employer paying for coaching requests the participant's full journal. |
| P2 | A participant wants help arranging transport and selects one appointment's time and pickup details to share. |
| P3 | A coach proposes using a personal cloud document for recovery notes. |
| P4 | A client accepts a coaching relationship; the coach assumes every past entry is now available. |
| P5 | A coach sends a note to the wrong recipient and proposes deleting their own copy without reporting the incident. |

Reviewer anchors: sponsorship and relationship acceptance do not create blanket
sharing rights. P2 still needs the exact recipient/purpose and approved mechanism.
P3 needs approved storage; P5 needs the established incident process and factual
record, not concealment. Do not promise that every consumer app is HIPAA covered.

## 3. Trauma-informed and culturally responsive engagement

Rewrite each statement to offer choice, explain purpose and avoid assumptions.
The participant may decline a topic or correct the coach's understanding.

| Case | Statement to revise |
|---|---|
| T1 | “You must describe the worst event before we can set a goal.” |
| T2 | “Your family will obviously handle that for you.” |
| T3 | “You agreed because you understood; there is no need to check.” |
| T4 | “Missing a session proves you are not committed.” |
| T5 | “My recovery path worked, so it is the right one for you.” |

Write a cultural-humility reflection: identify one assumption you might make
about family support, faith, disability, work or help-seeking; describe how you
would ask rather than assume; explain how you would respond to correction.
Do not request a real participant's trauma history for this exercise.

## 4. Communication skills

Convert each statement into an open question, accurate reflection, summary or
permission-based offer of information. Identify the technique and its purpose.
An acceptable response invites correction and does not hide a directive inside
a question. More than one response can be effective.

| Case | Statement to revise |
|---|---|
| C1 | “You should quit that job immediately.” |
| C2 | “Why can't you just make the call?” |
| C3 | “I know exactly how you feel.” |
| C4 | “Stop worrying and follow my plan.” |
| C5 | “The only useful support group is the one I use.” |
| C6 | “You did it because I kept pushing you.” |
| C7 | “There is no reason to be upset about that.” |
| C8 | “Let me tell you everything you need to do next.” |
| C9 | “You are resistant whenever you disagree with me.” |
| C10 | “If you were motivated, you would have finished.” |

Pair practice: one learner chooses a fictional practical goal; another coaches
for five minutes using listening, a reflection, an open question, an affirmation
of a specific effort and a participant-chosen next step. The observer records
the actual wording and whether the participant could correct or decline it.
Rotate roles. A text alternative may be used when needed; accessibility needs
must not be treated as a lack of coaching ability.

## 5. Goals, action planning and accountability

For each goal, draft a participant-led plan with one next step, chosen timing,
needed resources, a likely barrier, a smaller alternative, a confidence question
and a neutral follow-up question. Do not promise an outcome outside the person's
control. Ask the fictional participant to choose before treating the plan as agreed.

| Case | Starting goal |
|---|---|
| G1 | “I want to return to work, but transport is unreliable.” |
| G2 | “I coordinate care for three relatives and keep mixing up appointments.” |
| G3 | “I need stable housing and feel overwhelmed by the applications.” |

Rewrite: “If you miss this goal, I will record you as noncompliant and withhold
support.” Demonstrate a review of barriers and learning without threats.
For G2, a paper planning exercise does not prove that digital multi-person care
features exist. Keep each fictional person's details separate.

## 6. Documentation and shared progress

Rewrite each note using only facts actually provided. Identify reported versus
observed information, omit unnecessary details and identify what is unknown.
Do not invent a participant's consent, diagnosis, action or outcome.

| Case | Note to revise |
|---|---|
| D1 | “Participant is lazy.” Actual fact: they did not attend the scheduled session. |
| D2 | “Participant is manic.” Actual fact: they reported three hours of sleep and requested a shorter plan. |
| D3 | “Family is toxic.” Actual fact: participant asked to keep family outside the current sharing scope. |
| D4 | “Housing case is won.” Actual fact: participant booked a legal-aid appointment. |
| D5 | “Participant refuses help.” Actual fact: participant declined one suggested support group. |
| D6 | “Crisis resolved.” Actual fact: an approved handoff was initiated; its outcome is not yet known. |
| D7 | “All journal entries copied for continuity.” Actual fact: participant selected one goal summary for sharing. |
| D8 | “Original error removed so no one will know.” Actual fact: a date in a note needs correction through the approved process. |

Create a complete fictional session note with: date/context, participant-selected
purpose, reported or observed facts, agreed action, referral if applicable,
explicit sharing scope and next follow-up. Leave unknown fields marked unknown.
Reviewer checks the note against the fictional facts, not its apparent length.

## 7. Recognition, referral and escalation

These cases require qualified scope/safety review before teaching. Use the exact
reviewed organizational protocol; the workbook does not supply a substitute
clinical assessment or jurisdiction-specific reporting rule. Describe when to
pause ordinary coaching, the appropriate type of route, what information the
handoff needs and what the factual note should contain. Do not diagnose or make
a participant prove the seriousness of a concern. When immediate danger is
reported, do not delay the applicable emergency response for a coaching exercise.

| Case | Fictional situation |
|---|---|
| R1 | A participant wants to review a routine weekly goal and reports no immediate concern. |
| R2 | A participant requests a diagnosis for persistent low mood. |
| R3 | A participant reports immediate physical danger during a remote session. |
| R4 | A participant becomes unresponsive during a session. |
| R5 | A participant reports severe breathing difficulty. |
| R6 | A participant describes severe emotional distress and asks for crisis support. |
| R7 | A participant requests individualized advice about stopping medication. |
| R8 | A participant reports possible abuse of a dependent adult; applicable duties need confirmation. |
| R9 | A participant needs a public benefits eligibility decision. |
| R10 | A participant requests a housing placement guarantee. |
| R11 | A participant reports a possible overdose affecting someone nearby. |
| R12 | A participant asks for help organizing nonurgent questions for a clinician. |

Reviewer anchors: distinguish routine coaching, qualified professional/public
screener/nonprofit navigation, behavioral crisis support and emergency response.
R3/R4/R5/R11 contain urgent danger indicators; R6 calls for the approved crisis
pathway with immediate-danger escalation if indicated. R8 requires applicable
reporting/escalation guidance, not a rule borrowed from another jurisdiction.
For remote cases, the reviewer must verify how the actual safety protocol handles
location, disconnection and appropriate handoff. Do not mark an undefined protocol
as demonstrated. Prepare one fictional factual handoff note with unknowns explicit.

## 8. Capstone submission companion

Use the existing Jordan scenario and existing human-review rubric; neither is
replaced here. Submit these labeled sections so the reviewer can find the evidence:

1. Identify the participant's requests and separate ordinary coaching from
   clinical, legal, medication and immediate safety concerns.
2. State the first response and applicable reviewed safety handoff procedure.
   Explain which ordinary goal work must wait.
3. Give a respectful listening response that preserves choice and avoids diagnosis.
4. Specify the exact journal-sharing request, intended recipient, purpose and
   permission process; do not presume blanket consent or send real data.
5. After immediate concerns are addressed, describe how the participant may choose
   one manageable practical next step and an appropriate outside resource.
6. Write the factual note with actions, sharing decisions, unknowns and follow-up.
7. Cross-reference each existing rubric criterion to a passage in the submission.

Reviewer records observed evidence, required revisions and the existing critical
failure checks. Passing arithmetic cannot cancel a critical failure. No automatic
capstone pass, reviewer identity or certificate is created by this worksheet.

## Source boundaries

General coaching principles reference the existing course's ICF ethics and
competency sources. Privacy education references HHS consumer-app guidance and
the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule. These organizations did not author,
endorse or validate these original exercises. The course's existing SAMHSA sources
and the organization's actual protocol require source/scope review for section 7.
This workbook supplies no ICF credential, continuing-education approval or license.
