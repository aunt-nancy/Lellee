Privacy Policy

How Lellee handles sensitive recovery, account, journal, support, notification and resource information.

Qualified legal review required before public launch.

This is a counsel-review candidate built around Lellee's current consumer architecture and current official privacy guidance. It is not a substitute for advice from an attorney who has reviewed Lellee's entity structure, contracts, vendors, states served, and actual data flows.

1. Information Lellee handles

Depending on the features you use, Lellee may handle account information; recovery date and goals; recovery pathway and optional belief preferences; daily check-ins; cravings, mood, stress and guided responses; journal entries; triggers and coping tools; milestones; support contacts you enter; saved meetings and resources; reminders and browser push-subscription information; city/state preferences; and subscription status if paid services are activated.

Lellee also receives limited technical information needed to authenticate accounts, secure the service, deliver requested features, investigate errors and prevent abuse.

2. How Lellee uses information

Lellee uses information to provide the features you request, maintain your private recovery history, calculate Journey timing, save entries and preferences, provide user-selected personalization, deliver reminders you enable, protect accounts, troubleshoot the service and fulfill privacy requests.

Advertising boundary

Lellee's consumer launch policy is not to use journals, cravings, recovery dates, check-ins, pathways, support contacts or similar private recovery information for targeted advertising. Sponsored directory placements must be labeled and do not receive a user's private recovery history merely because they paid for placement.

3. Personalization

Personalization is designed to use information within your Lellee account to decide what recovery content to surface first. It is not a clinical diagnosis or relapse-risk prediction. Optional personalization can be turned off.

4. Service providers

Lellee uses vendors to operate the service. The current architecture uses Supabase for authentication, database and server functions; Vercel for web hosting; browser/device push services for notifications a user enables; and Stripe if paid billing is later activated. Before launch, Lellee should maintain a current vendor register, limit each vendor to an appropriate purpose, and review contractual/privacy settings.

5. Sharing and disclosures

Lellee does not make private journals or recovery histories publicly visible by default. User-directed exports and Recovery Story editions are controlled by the user. Information may be disclosed to service providers as needed to operate requested features or when disclosure is legally required. Requests involving substance-use information require heightened review.

6. Consumer health information

Recovery and substance-use information can qualify as consumer health or medical information under federal or state law. Lellee therefore treats the information as highly sensitive even when a particular law may not apply to every account or feature. See the separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Notice.

7. HIPAA

HIPAA applicability depends on Lellee's legal relationship to a covered entity. A consumer app is not automatically a HIPAA covered entity. If Lellee creates, receives, maintains or transmits protected health information on behalf of a covered entity, a business-associate relationship and additional requirements may apply. Provider-facing use must be reviewed separately before launch.

8. Substance-use treatment records / 42 CFR Part 2

Self-directed consumer use does not by itself make Lellee a Part 2 program. If Lellee later operates for a Part 2 program, receives Part 2 records, or supports regulated provider workflows, additional confidentiality, consent, notice, security and breach requirements must be implemented.

9. Your requests

Signed-in users can submit access, export, correction, deletion, limitation, withdrawal and consumer-health-data requests in Privacy & Data. Lellee may verify identity before disclosing or deleting sensitive information.

10. Retention and deletion

Lellee should retain identifiable information only as long as reasonably necessary for the requested service, account security, legal obligations and user-directed history. Deletion workflows must account for active systems, processors and applicable backup requirements. The operational retention schedule in this build must be finalized with counsel and actual vendor capabilities.

11. Security

Lellee uses authenticated accounts, row-level database controls, HTTPS, server-side secrets, restricted administrative controls and other technical safeguards. No system guarantees absolute security. Incidents involving recovery information require immediate containment, investigation and legal breach-notification assessment.

12. Age

The recommended consumer launch posture in this build is age 18+. Do not launch a minor/youth version under these terms without a separate review of age, consent, parent/guardian access, minor-consented services, substance-use confidentiality and state law.

13. Material changes

Material changes should receive a new legal-document version and, where appropriate, a new user notice or acceptance request.

Lellee — private, practical recovery support for today and the long view. The consumer service is not an emergency service, licensed treatment program, or substitute for individualized medical or behavioral-health care.
