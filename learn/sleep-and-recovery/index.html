Sleep and Substance-Use Recovery

Sleep and recovery can affect one another. Persistent sleep problems may deserve professional evaluation rather than self-treatment alone.

Lellee Editorial Team · Educational recovery content

Start with the person, not the rule

Recovery is highly personal. Use this information as a way to understand options and make a safer next decision, not as a test of whether recovery is being done the “right” way.

Sleep can be disrupted during change

Stress, routines, health conditions, medications and substance-related effects can all influence sleep.

Use basic sleep-supporting habits

Regular timing, reducing late stimulation and creating a calmer sleep environment may help some people.

Do not self-medicate sleep problems

Persistent or severe sleep problems should be discussed with an appropriate healthcare professional, especially when medications or withdrawal may be involved.

Common questions

Does everyone need the same recovery approach?

No. Recovery can involve different combinations of professional care, peer support, mutual-help, medication, skills, faith or spirituality, and personal supports.

Can self-guided recovery tools replace treatment?

No. Self-guided tools can support recovery but are not substitutes for needed medical, behavioral-health, withdrawal, or emergency care.
