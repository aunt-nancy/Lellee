Faith and Spirituality in Recovery

Faith or spirituality can be meaningful recovery supports for people who choose them and can coexist with other recovery approaches.

Lellee Editorial Team · Educational recovery content

Start with the person, not the rule

Recovery is highly personal. Use this information as a way to understand options and make a safer next decision, not as a test of whether recovery is being done the “right” way.

Belief should be a preference, not a requirement

Some people want explicitly faith-based recovery content; others prefer spiritual, secular, mixed or exploratory approaches.

Faith can support connection and meaning

Congregations, spiritual practices, values and community can be sources of support when they are safe and personally meaningful.

Clinical and medical needs remain separate

Faith preferences should never override emergency, medical or behavioral-health safety guidance.

Common questions

Does everyone need the same recovery approach?

No. Recovery can involve different combinations of professional care, peer support, mutual-help, medication, skills, faith or spirituality, and personal supports.

Can self-guided recovery tools replace treatment?

No. Self-guided tools can support recovery but are not substitutes for needed medical, behavioral-health, withdrawal, or emergency care.
