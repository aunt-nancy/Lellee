What to Do After a Return to Substance Use

A return to use can be a reason to reconnect with support, assess immediate medical safety and re-engage with recovery rather than disappear from it.

Lellee Editorial Team · Educational recovery content

Start with the person, not the rule

Recovery is highly personal. Use this information as a way to understand options and make a safer next decision, not as a test of whether recovery is being done the “right” way.

Check immediate safety first

Depending on the substance, amount, tolerance and circumstances, medical risk can be serious. Seek appropriate emergency or medical help when needed.

Reconnect quickly

Contact a treatment provider, recovery support, trusted person or other appropriate help rather than waiting for shame to disappear.

Use the event as information

When safe, review what happened beforehand: environment, stress, isolation, access, expectations, health or changes in routine. The goal is to improve the next plan.

Common questions

Does everyone need the same recovery approach?

No. Recovery can involve different combinations of professional care, peer support, mutual-help, medication, skills, faith or spirituality, and personal supports.

Can self-guided recovery tools replace treatment?

No. Self-guided tools can support recovery but are not substitutes for needed medical, behavioral-health, withdrawal, or emergency care.
