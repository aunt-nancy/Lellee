Loneliness in Recovery

Loneliness can appear even when recovery is going well. Intentional, safe connection can be part of protecting recovery.

Lellee Editorial Team · Educational recovery content

Start with the person, not the rule

Recovery is highly personal. Use this information as a way to understand options and make a safer next decision, not as a test of whether recovery is being done the “right” way.

Loneliness is not the same as being alone

Someone can feel lonely in a crowd or feel peaceful while spending time alone. Notice what kind of connection is actually missing.

Use low-pressure connection

A short call, meeting, class, peer group, faith community, volunteer activity or shared routine can be easier than trying to build deep relationships immediately.

Protect against unsafe connection

Wanting connection does not require returning to relationships or environments that threaten safety or recovery.

Common questions

Does everyone need the same recovery approach?

No. Recovery can involve different combinations of professional care, peer support, mutual-help, medication, skills, faith or spirituality, and personal supports.

Can self-guided recovery tools replace treatment?

No. Self-guided tools can support recovery but are not substitutes for needed medical, behavioral-health, withdrawal, or emergency care.
