Medication-Supported Recovery

Medication can be an evidence-based component of an individualized substance-use treatment and recovery plan.

Lellee Editorial Team · Educational recovery content

Start with the person, not the rule

Recovery is highly personal. Use this information as a way to understand options and make a safer next decision, not as a test of whether recovery is being done the “right” way.

Medication and recovery are not opposites

For some substance-use disorders, medications are part of appropriate medical treatment and can be combined with other supports.

Treatment decisions are individualized

Medication choice, risks, benefits, dosing and duration belong with qualified healthcare professionals rather than a self-guided app.

Recovery can include several supports

People may combine medication with counseling, peer support, meetings, housing support, family support and other recovery approaches.

Common questions

Does everyone need the same recovery approach?

No. Recovery can involve different combinations of professional care, peer support, mutual-help, medication, skills, faith or spirituality, and personal supports.

Can self-guided recovery tools replace treatment?

No. Self-guided tools can support recovery but are not substitutes for needed medical, behavioral-health, withdrawal, or emergency care.
